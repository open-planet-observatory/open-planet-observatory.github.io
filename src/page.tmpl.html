Live cams

Watch the planet, not the people

Official cameras pointed at volcanoes, wildlife, the sky and Earth from orbit. Each card opens the owner's own page; nothing is re-streamed or recorded here. Some cams are seasonal or only light up in daylight.

Stories from NASA

Official NASA channels, each paired with the layer it explains. Specific videos can be added as cards here; NASA's own videos are public domain, embedded or linked under YouTube's rules (never behind a paywall, never with ads on the player).

A cam is listed only if

  • It's run by or with the owner of the place: a science agency, park, observatory, aquarium or nonprofit
  • It points at nature, the sky, space or a science site
  • People in view are too small or far away to be identified
  • We link to it rather than copy, store or re-stream the video

Never listed

  • Street, traffic, city-square and business cams
  • Private or unsecured cameras found by scanning the internet
  • Beach, campus or venue cams where faces or plates are readable
  • Any cam in or near an active conflict zone

If something changes

  • A cam whose view changes to show identifiable people is removed
  • Owners can ask for removal at any time, no questions asked
  • Spotted a problem? Report it on the project's issue tracker

News attention follows the big quake

English-language articles per day mentioning each hazard (GDELT). The M7.0 quake near Nagqu, China, struck late on Oct 22 UTC; earthquake coverage peaked the next day.

Who is in orbit

Tracked active satellites by constellation (CelesTrak groups). One company's broadband network outnumbers everything else combined.

Where aircraft data comes from

Airborne aircraft seen by OpenSky's volunteer receivers, by country of registration. The U.S. leads partly because that's where most receivers are. Coverage gaps over oceans and Africa reflect receivers, not empty skies.

Design principles

Built to inform, not to surveil

Dashboards like OSIRIS stack public camera feeds and network scanners next to the science. This one draws the line on purpose.

Places, not people

Aircraft are shown as counts per region, never as individual tracks. The only cameras are official nature, space and science cams where no one can be identified; no street cameras, no license-plate readers.

OSIRIS shows 2,000+ live traffic cameras.

Every number has a source

Each layer names its provider, update rate and license, and links to the original.

Several OSIRIS layers are hand-typed static lists shown as if live.

Show the blind spots

Receiver coverage, sensor limits and news-language bias are stated where they shape what you see.

A map with no caveats looks more complete than the data is.

Read-only, always

Nothing here probes, scans or contacts any system. It only reads published feeds.

OSIRIS ships a port and vulnerability scanner.

Acceptable use

Research and education only

You may

  • Study patterns, teach, write and research using what's shown here
  • Follow the links to the original sources and use their data under their own terms
  • Share the page for learning

You may not

  • Use anything here to locate, follow, target or harass a person or group
  • Treat it as an emergency alert or a basis for tactical, military or safety decisions
  • Republish source data in ways its license forbids

How this page handles you

  • No sign-up, no tracking scripts, no visitor logs kept by this page
  • Notable deaths are listed only with a cited date, an English Wikipedia article and at least 48 hours' delay
  • Who is behind a cyber attack is stated only when a government advisory states it; breach lists name the breached organisation, never victims
  • Mass shootings are listed as the source lists them, without names of victims or shooters, with a note on how definitions differ
  • Spot an error? Follow the source link: corrections belong with the original publisher
How this prototype works

From prototype to live site

How the data stays fresh

  • On the public site, a scheduled job collects every feed every 10 minutes (slow-changing ones hourly or daily) and publishes the files this page reads. Its run logs are public.
  • Earthquakes are fetched straight from USGS by your browser every minute.
  • If a source fails, the last good data stays up, with its own timestamp.
  • Satellite positions and the day/night line are calculated live in your browser from orbital elements and the sun's position.
  • Aircraft and fire counts are grouped into map cells, which keeps the page small and the detail appropriate.

Open source

  • The page, the collectors and the publishing workflow are all in one public repository, licensed AGPL-3.0.
  • Anyone can run their own copy for free; improvements to a public copy must stay open too.
  • Every source is credited, and its own license applies to its data.

Not included, on purpose

  • Public CCTV, street and traffic cameras, license-plate readers
  • Tracking named people's private jets
  • Conflict data with non-commercial licenses (ACLED)
  • Network scanning or "recon" tools
  • Scraping Google search results (against its terms) and leaked data itself: only the fact of a breach, as catalogued by HIBP
